Patterns / Feedback

Feedback

Say what happened, close to where it happened, and only interrupt for what cannot be undone. Most actions act at once and offer an Undo; a confirmation is the exception.

Which one

Which feedback to use
When Use Example
Something just happened Toast Layout saved. Order PO-2210 created.
It can be taken back Toast with Undo Inventory removed from your overview.
It cannot be taken back Dialog, before acting Reset the overview? Discard the new order?
A state that lasts Notice, in place 2 invoices overdue. The carrier changed.
A field is wrong Error under the field Choose a date from May 12 on.

States that last

Loading

The product waits in two places, and each shows it in place, with the words of what is happening rather than an empty shape. While the search reads a query, its status line says “Understanding “…”…” and a light band in the accent green crosses the words once a second; the results replace it. While the AI assistant writes an answer, three dots pulse in its bubble, 1 s each, 150 ms apart. Cards arrive with their data, so the overview has no skeleton screens; a module that someday needs to load reuses the shimmer on its own words. With reduced motion requested, the words stay still and the dots stop.

Writing it

  • Past tense for what happened (“Layout saved.”), a question for a decision (“Reset the overview?”).
  • Name the thing: “Inventory removed”, not “Item removed”.
  • The button that answers a question repeats its verb: Reset, Discard. Never Yes or OK.
  • No exclamation marks and no apologies. Errors say how to fix them.
