Patterns / Editing the overview

Editing the overview

People choose which cards their overview shows, where, and at what size. On desktops and tablets they drag the cards themselves; on phones the overview becomes a list. Every edit is a draft until it is saved, and saving can be undone.

As a list, on phones

On desktops and tablets

  • Real size. On a 1440 px desktop the overview has three columns of 370 px with 20 px between them and 32 px margins; on an 820 px tablet, the 60 px rail, 24 px margins and two columns of 346 px. The demo draws them at 1:1 and scrolls sideways when the page is narrower; Fit to width draws the whole canvas smaller with one zoom, like the product, never each card.
  • Edit layout turns the overview into a draft: each card gets a dashed edge, a handle on its top edge to move it, a remove button outside its top end corner and a handle on its bottom edge to resize it, with its size beside it. A bar rises at the foot: “Editing layout. Drag a card by its top handle. Pull the bottom handle to change its size.”, with Reset to default, Cancel and Save layout. Every state a card goes through is drawn on Cards.
  • Moving. A card lifts while dragged: it grows to 103 % in 200 ms and rises to elevation 4. The others make room in 320 ms; its drop placeholder shows where it will land. Dropped, it glides into its slot in 500 ms on the expressive spring.
  • Resizing steps through the sizes the card offers, never in between, and the card eases to its new height in 320 ms; a label over the handle names the size and says when the columns end level.
  • Tablets take their layout from the desktop’s: cards are read top to bottom, then left to right, and each goes into the shorter column. Handles grow to 44 × 20 px and the remove button to 28 px. When the number of columns changes, every card travels to its new place in 620 ms on cubic-bezier(.2,0,0,1).
  • New element waits at the foot of the shortest column: it brings back the cards that were removed.
  • Touch uses the same handles with a larger hit area; the handles of two neighboring cards share the gap between them.
  • Keyboard. Every handle is a button: Space picks a card up, the arrow keys move it, Space drops it, Escape puts it back.

Rules

  • A draft until saved. Save layout stays off until something changes. Cancel leaves at once when nothing changed, and asks before it discards changes.
  • Save, then Undo. Saving does not ask; its toast offers to undo.
  • Reset to default asks first. It puts every card back in its place and size; the draft can still be canceled.
  • Each size class keeps its own layout once edited there; what is shown is shared. Hiding a card on a phone hides it everywhere.
  • Moves are announced. “Inventory moved to position 2 of 6.”
