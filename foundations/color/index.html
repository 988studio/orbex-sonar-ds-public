Foundations / Color

Color

A brand green, three status hues and a family of green-tinted neutrals. Components never use the palette directly: they use roles, and every role that carries text is measured against the surfaces it sits on.

How the palette is built

Brand and status colors are anchors, exact values chosen by design. When a role needs another tone of an anchor, for instance red text that must reach 4.5:1, that tone is derived: same hue and chroma, different lightness. Derived tones are outlined below.

Neutrals are generated. They are the brand green at very low chroma, from one formula. The hue drifts slightly from dark to light, as the hand-picked originals did, and the chroma eases off near black and white, where tint reads as dirt. Every tone is named by its CIELAB lightness L*, from 0 (black) to 100 (white), so the distance between two names predicts their contrast.

Two neutral families are anchors instead: neutral-dark holds the product’s exact dark surfaces, lines and grays, and neutral-light the few light-theme grays that do not fall on the formula.

Text and icons

Emphasis levels, defined by contrast so both themes read alike. Every level meant to be read passes WCAG AA (4.5:1) on every surface, containers, selected rows and menus included. Icons use the same levels as the text beside them.

Placeholder text is text: WCAG makes no exception for it, so it reads at the tertiary level. It keeps a role of its own so fields can change it without touching tertiary. Disabled is exempt in WCAG and takes the product’s own gray; the control keeps its outline or shape, so it still reads as present.

Muted is body text one step quieter than default. Meta is for dates, units and supporting figures: in dark it is the product’s own gray, quieter than tertiary. In light the product’s gray, #737D71, reaches only 4.21:1 on a card and 3.73:1 on the page, below 4.5:1, so meta takes the tertiary tone, #5E685D: 5.71:1 on a card, 5.05:1 on the page.

Colored text uses darker tones than the fills of the same hue in light, and lighter ones in dark. It sits on cards, pages, menus and rows; on containers, status is shown with an icon or a chip.

Text that is not read

  • Never lower contrast with opacity, with one exception kept from the product: a chart’s running-total toggle fades to 40 % when off. Its state is also given by aria-pressed and by its line leaving the chart.
  • Neighboring days are context, not content. A month calendar fills its six rows with the days of the months around it in the disabled gray; each of those days reads at full contrast in its own month.
  • Decorative previews are hidden from assistive technology. A glimpse of the next day at the edge of a scrolling view carries aria-hidden="true" and no focusable content. It is exempt from contrast because the same content is one swipe away, at full contrast.

Where the system departs from the product

The rule: the system takes the product’s colors unless a pair fails WCAG AA, and then accessibility wins. These are the roles where the product’s color fails and the system’s replaces it, with both ratios measured on the card (#FDFDFC in light, #1F251F in dark). Danger in dark follows the same rule: marks and fills keep the product’s #F04B4B, red text takes #F97068, and a red fill that carries white text takes #DD3535.
